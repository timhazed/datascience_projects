543 MODULE 18: Assessment, monitoring and evaluation 1.Use of triangulation and mixed methods: No one tool will provide all the information and complementary use of tools, quantitative and qualitative methods will be important. There can also be difficulty due to resource and ethical reasons to apply a strictly experimental design. 2. Getting management and stakeholder ownership and engagement in the monitoring and evaluation pro- cess. Often monitoring and evaluation is seen as a standalone reporting task. There is a need to internalize project management and staff responsibilities more. 3.There is a need to build capacity across the board on planning and monitoring and evaluation, and create a learning culture. CSA projects are often experiments, and need to maximize lesson learning for potential scaling up. 4.Networks and shared learning between projects and programmes is critical in rapidly sharing practi- cal knowledge on monitoring and evaluation (examples of networks on monitoring and evaluation are www.3ieimpact.org, http://mande.co.uk, etc.) and similar sharing networks have considerable potential for monitoring and evaluation for CSA.
